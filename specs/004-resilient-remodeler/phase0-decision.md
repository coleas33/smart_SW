Risk **RK-1** (`plan.md`, Risks) says: *`ReorderFeature` cannot move a feature past a dependency, so
stage 1 may change almost nothing on a real part.* Phase 0 exists to turn that sentence into a number

writes to no document, calls no language model and needs no SOLIDWORKS seat.

## 3. The honest reading

### 3.1 Not one of these parts is real

Every row in section 2.1 came out of a Python builder. The corpus-A builders are the same ones the
planner's own unit tests use; the corpus-B builders were written for feature 003 to seed one violation
of each RMS rule. A synthetic tree's dependency graph is whatever its author declared it to be.
**These numbers measure that the planner is self-consistent. They do not measure how re-modelable a
real part is, and they cannot close RK-1.** T003 is the only measurement in this feature that bears on
RK-1, and it has not been taken.

### 3.2 The aggregate fraction is not a score, and on a refused part it is not even work

0.752 across the corpus is arithmetic over a bag of fixtures chosen to exercise code paths, not a
sample of anything. Worse, seven of the 22 rows are **refused** parts, and a refused part still prints
a fraction: `frame.SLDPRT` shows 14 of 14 = 1.000 while planning **zero** changes, because the
fraction is measured before the folder refusal is applied. On any row whose state is `failed`, read
the fraction as *the fraction the part would have had* and read the `chg` column — which is 0 — as
what the re-modeler would actually do. FR-009 and the US2 success criteria already forbid a threshold
on this fraction; this is the concrete reason why.

### 3.3 Where the corpus is closest to a real part, the number is 0.28 — and the rebuild list is mostly contagion

`rms-part` `doc:3` `bracket.SLDPRT` is the largest and most deliberately messy tree available:
25 content features, hand-designed. It yields **7 of 25 reaching their target group, 7 moves, and 20
features on the rebuild list**. That is exactly the shape RK-1 predicted, and it is worth reading
carefully, because 20 is not 20 independent problems:

- 1 real pin — `Boss-Extrude5` is a `3-Core` feature that depends on `Cut-Extrude4` in `4-Detail`;
- 1 shared sketch — `Sketch3` is consumed by both `Boss-Extrude2` and `Boss-Extrude3`;
- 2 ambiguous names — two folder rows both literally named `3-Core`;
- **16 `splits_group` entries** — every other member of `3-Core` and `4-Detail`, listed because those
  two groups cannot be made contiguous once the four problems above sit inside the span.

So a handful of genuine blockers put two thirds of the tree on the rebuild list. If stage 1 ships, the
report must not present 20 rows as 20 problems; the honest sentence for this part is *"two dependency
problems and two duplicate names make `3-Core` and `4-Detail` unfoldable, so 16 further features
cannot be grouped"*. **A run that moves 7 of 25 features says "this part needs rebuilding", not
"success".** That is the stated expectation of `spec.md` ("the reorganizable fraction may be small …
stage 1's product is a partition with reasons") and of RK-1's mitigation in `plan.md`, and the offline
corpus is consistent with it.

### 3.4 Every scope verdict here is `unresolved`, by design

Multibody, weldment, sheet metal, mesh/graphics body and 3D Interconnect are COM readings
`remodel.probe_scope` takes from the engineer's open document. A package carries none of them, so the
dry run reports each one unread and the gate `unresolved` — an unread signal is never a pass. The six
`remodel-refusal-*` signal fixtures therefore **plan cleanly through the CLI** (rows 8–13) and refuse
only under the golden harness, which supplies the `ScopeSignals` from each fixture's `case.json`.
Nothing in section 2 says any of those parts would pass scope on a workstation.

### 3.5 The gate that actually stops these parts is not `ReorderFeature` — it is the RMS-named-folder refusal

**6 of the 9 parts from feature 003's fixtures refuse before anything is copied**, on
`rms_named_folder_wrong_members` (17 refusals in total). Not one of them refuses because of a
dependency. They refuse because they already carry folders named `1-Ref`, `3-Core`, `5-Modify`,
`6-Quarantine` and so on whose membership does not match what the planner computes, and v1 has no
dissolve path — `IModelDoc2.EditDelete` is deliberately not in the stage-1 allowlist (owner decision,
FR-007).

This matters more than the corpus size suggests. The population most likely to want a re-modeler is
the population that has *started* organizing by hand, and those are precisely the parts that carry a
group-named folder holding almost-but-not-quite the right members. On this corpus that refusal removes
two thirds of the analyzable parts before RK-1 gets a chance to matter. **T003 should be read for this
as much as for RK-1**: if the owner's real parts also refuse here, the go / no-go is not only about
`ReorderFeature`, it is about whether v1's no-dissolve rule leaves stage 1 with any subject at all.

### 3.6 Descriptions, globals and equations are untested by this run

Revision 1 proposes no description and no global by design (the US3 judgement phase is Phase 7 and has
not been written), and **every package in the corpus carries 0 equation rows**. The coverage notes do
record where a global *could* be justified from feature data — 1 on `remodel-unplaceable`, 1 on
`remodel-refusal-rms-folder`, 2 on `rms-part` `frame`, 3 on `rms-part` `bracket`, 2 on
`rms-exceptions` `housing`, 1 on `rms-exceptions` `cover` — but nothing here measures the intent half
of stage 1. This table is about reorganizing only.

### 3.7 Nothing offline exercises the scale the order planner was built for

The largest move count anywhere is 7, on `bracket.SLDPRT` — a part that is refused, so those 7 moves
would never be attempted. The largest move count on a part that actually plans is **3**
(`remodel-reversed`, a 6-feature tree with no dependencies). FR-011's "a 200-feature part is not moved
200 times to achieve 30 moves" is asserted as minimality in the unit tests, but the planner has never
met a tree of that size in this corpus. A real part of 150–250 features is the first thing T003 will
put through it.

### 3.8 Two reporting defects found by this run (reported, not fixed)

Both are in shipped Phase 1 code and both make the dry run's own output misleading. Neither changes any
number in section 2. They are recorded here because this file is the first consumer of that output,
and a Phase 0 decision taken off a misleading sentence is a bad decision.

1. **`reviewer/src/swreview/cli.py`, `_remodel_refusals`** — the folder refusal message is built from
   member *counts*, so when the counts happen to be equal it reads as a contradiction. On
   `remodel-refusal-rms-folder` it prints *"the folder 3-Core (feat:0002) holds 3 feature(s) where the
   method puts 3"*, while the actual members are `feat:0003, feat:0004, feat:0005` and the expected
   members are `feat:0001, feat:0003, feat:0006`. The refusal is correct — membership is compared on
   ids, as T018 requires — but the sentence explaining it is not. It should name the unexpected and
   the missing members, which is also what FR-007 asks for ("naming the folder and the unexpected
   members").
2. **`reviewer/src/swreview/remodel/plan.py`, `_why_changes`** — the function branches on
   `scope.refused` and `folders.refused` but not on a dependency cycle, so a cycle-refused part falls
   through to the final sentence and reports *"no change is planned, and nothing refused this part:
   the tree is already in the order the method wants and every group folder it needs already holds
   exactly its features"*. `remodel-cycle` prints exactly that while its own `refusals[]` carries
   `cycle`. The function's own docstring says "The two are never the same sentence"; for a cycle they
   currently are.

**The question**: *staged* (build stage 1 reorganize now, re-specify stage 2 rebuild after stage 1 has
run on real parts) versus *rebuild-only* (do not build stage 1's write path at all; keep the Phase 1
planner as the shipped deliverable and re-specify the feature as a rebuild advisor).

The evidence that points each way, written down before the numbers arrive so the decision is not made
by whichever number happens to look best:

**Points toward staged (go)**

- The real parts reach a meaningful fraction — features actually move, and the moves are the ones an
  engineer would have made by hand.
- The rebuild lists are dominated by a small number of genuine blockers with a large `splits_group`
  tail, so naming those few blockers is itself useful output.
- Few or no real parts refuse on `rms_named_folder_wrong_members`, so stage 1 has a subject.
- The scope gate does not refuse most of the owner's parts (multibody, weldment, sheet metal).

**Points toward rebuild-only (no-go on stage 1's write path)**

- The real parts move almost nothing — the "3 of 200" case. Stage 1 would ship a write path, a write
  guard, a geometry gate and a rollback engine in order to produce a change list an engineer would not
  act on.
- Most real parts refuse before a copy is even made, on `rms_named_folder_wrong_members` or on a scope
  signal. Stage 1 has no subject.
- The Phase 1 planner already answers the question engineers actually ask — "how re-modelable is this
  part, and what is blocking it?" — without any of Phases 2 to 9, and the marginal value of applying
  the moves is small next to the marginal risk of a tool that writes to a document.

**A third option to hold open**: *planner ships, write path deferred*. Release `swreview remodel plan`
and the Model check tab's use of it as the deliverable, and defer the decision on Phases 2 to 9 until
there is evidence about how often engineers act on the planner's report. This costs nothing already
built and leaves the constitution's write exception unexercised.
